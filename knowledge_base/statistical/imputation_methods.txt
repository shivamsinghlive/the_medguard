Missing Data and Imputation
Reviewed: 2026-10-24. Every claim cites an entry in knowledge_base/SOURCES.md.

The missing-data terminology used here (including "missing at random") traces to Rubin's 1976 paper on inference with missing data. [S7]
"Missing at random" is an assumption that justifies an analysis, not a property that can be read from the data. [S8]
Analyses restricted to complete cases may be biased. [S8]

Sources:
[S7] Rubin DB. Inference and missing data. Biometrika 1976;63(3):581-592. https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581
[S8] Sterne JAC et al. Multiple imputation for missing data in epidemiological and clinical research: potential and pitfalls. BMJ 2009;338:b2393. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b2393
